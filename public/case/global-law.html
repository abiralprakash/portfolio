Practice brand, not a brochure template.

Global Law Associates needed a digital presence that matched the seriousness of IP and corporate work — clear practice areas, professional authority, and space for legal knowledge publishing — without feeling like a generic firm theme.

Challenge

Authority without noise.

Law firm sites often collapse into dense menus or stock photography. The challenge was to present IP, corporate, litigation, and foreign investment work with clarity — and leave room for knowledge publishing that builds long-term trust.

Solution

Practice clarity with room to publish.

Global Law Associates received a corporate site with clear practice-area pathways — IP, corporate, litigation, foreign investment — professional people and firm narrative, and a publishing surface for legal knowledge without crowding the primary conversion path.

The live site at globallaw.com.np supports firm communications; the practice also operates IP desks on NepalIPMS.

User Story

From template firm site to practice clarity.

Before

After

Decision Log

Authority through structure.

Distinct practice pathways over a single services page

Decision: Separate entry points for IP, corporate, litigation, and investment advisory — not one undifferentiated “Our Services” scroll.

Reason: Visitors arrive with a specific legal question; hunting through generic copy erodes credibility before the first meeting.

Tradeoff: More pages to maintain; faster orientation for high-intent visitors.

Editorial visual language over template firm themes

Decision: Calm hierarchy, typographic authority, and restrained imagery instead of stock-photo hero templates.

Reason: Premium legal brands are judged on seriousness in the first ten seconds — visual noise reads as commodity.

Tradeoff: Less off-the-shelf theme speed; a site that matches practice standards.

Maintainable static front-end over heavy CMS lock-in

Decision: Ship a performance-minded, firm-owned front-end rather than a plugin-heavy CMS the team would fight daily.

Reason: Firms need content ownership without fighting their own publishing stack — launch polish matters less than ongoing control.

Tradeoff: Less drag-and-drop for non-technical editors; faster pages and fewer breaking updates.

Failed Attempts

Layouts that did not ship.

Mega-menu with every service nested. An early IA pass listed every sub-specialty in a hover menu. Visitors lost orientation within seconds — practice clarity required fewer, stronger pathways instead of exhaustive taxonomy.

Stock photography hero treatment. Generic handshake and skyline imagery undermined the premium positioning the firm needed. We moved to editorial typography and authentic firm narrative instead of visual drama.

Lessons learned

Authority is editorial, not decorative.

Law firm sites fail when they optimize for visual drama over practice clarity. Separating practice pathways, people, and publishing let the firm communicate expertise without a dense menu or generic template feel.

A maintainable front-end mattered as much as launch-day polish — firms need ownership of content without fighting their own CMS.
