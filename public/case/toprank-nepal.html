Challenge

Desktop-era content meeting phone-era students.

Much exam-prep material in Nepal still assumes long desktop sessions or physical coaching schedules. Aspirants need a product that respects fragmented time, works in Nepali and English, and makes the next study action obvious.

The UX had to feel like a learning tool, not a news portal — low chrome, clear hierarchy, and navigation that matches thumb-driven habits.

Solution

A daily study surface on the phone.

TopRank Nepal wraps PSC preparation in a mobile-first learning shell — home, search, study, notices, profile — with bilingual Nepali/English UI and content organized for progressive study rather than an undifferentiated article dump.

The public platform at topranknepal.com meets aspirants where they already prepare: short sessions on phones between work and coaching.

User Story

From PDF scavenging to daily study surface.

Before

PSC aspirants hunted prep material across Telegram groups and coaching handouts — PDFs with no search, no progress signal, and exam notices scattered across unofficial channels. Most content assumed long desktop sessions.

After

A mobile-first learning shell — home, search, study, notices, profile — with bilingual Nepali/English UI. Study paths replace scavenging; exam schedules stay in-product so learners do not leave mid-session.

Decision Log

Designed for fragmented time.

Mobile-first bottom navigation over desktop sidebar

Decision: Primary navigation as a thumb-driven bottom bar — home, search, study, notices, profile.

Reason: PSC prep happens in short phone sessions between work and coaching; desktop sidebars felt foreign on the device students actually use.

Tradeoff: Less horizontal space for chrome on small screens; familiar patterns that reduce orientation time.

Bilingual UI from day one

Decision: Ship Nepali and English labels across the learning shell, not English-only with translation deferred.

Reason: Language friction at the first tap kills daily habit formation for a large segment of aspirants.

Tradeoff: More copy and QA surface; lower drop-off at session start.

In-app notices over external channel dependency

Decision: Keep exam schedules and operational updates inside the product instead of pointing to Telegram groups.

Reason: Context-switching to unofficial notice channels broke study flow and eroded trust in schedule accuracy.

Tradeoff: Team must maintain notice content; learners stay in one learning context.

Failed Attempts

Desktop assumptions we corrected.

Desktop-first layout retrofitted to mobile. An early responsive pass kept sidebar navigation and dense two-column study views. On phones the hierarchy felt wrong — thumb reach and session length demanded a ground-up mobile shell instead.

Feature-rich dashboard homepage. We tried surfacing stats, recommendations, and multiple entry points on login. Short study sessions overwhelmed; simplifying to one obvious next action per tab improved return visits.

Lessons learned

Respect fragmented time.

PSC aspirants rarely sit at a desk for two-hour sessions. Designing for short, repeatable study actions on mobile — with bilingual labels and in-app notices — mattered more than feature-rich desktop layouts.

Keeping exam updates inside the product reduced context-switching to Telegram groups and unofficial notice channels.
